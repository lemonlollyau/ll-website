Schema markup for local businesses, explained simply

By the lemonlolly team·25 July 2026·5 min read

"Schema markup" is one of those phrases that makes a small business owner's eyes glaze over. It sounds like something for developers, or a box a fancy agency ticks to look busy. It's neither — and once you see what it's actually for, it's one of the more sensible half-hour jobs on your website.

So here's the plain version, with no code you need to understand and no jargon we can't explain in a sentence.

What schema markup actually is

Think of the label on the back of a tin. To you the tin is obviously baked beans, but the shop's system can't see that — the barcode is what tells the till "this is beans, this is the price, this is the brand." Schema markup is the barcode for your web page. It's a small bit of hidden text that labels the things on the page so Google knows exactly what it's looking at.

Without it, Google reads your contact page and makes an educated guess that the numbers near the top are probably your phone and opening hours. With it, you're telling Google flat out: this is the business name, this is the phone, these are the hours, this is the suburb. No guessing.

Schema doesn't change what your visitors see. It changes what Google understands. Those are two different audiences, and this one only talks to the robots.

What it does for a local business

Here's the honest bit first: adding schema won't shove you up the rankings on its own. It's not a magic ranking lever, and anyone selling it as one is fibbing. What it does is make you eligible for the richer, bigger listings that catch the eye — and a bigger listing gets more clicks even from the same position.

The things it can unlock for a local business:

  • Star ratings showing under your listing, pulled from your reviews.
  • The business info panel — name, hours, phone, map pin — shown cleanly on the right of search.
  • FAQ drop-downs right in the results, so your answers show before someone even clicks.
  • Event, product or service details where they apply, laid out neatly instead of buried in a paragraph.

None of that is guaranteed — Google decides what to show — but you can't win a raffle you didn't enter. Schema is the ticket.

The snippets actually worth adding

You don't need every type of schema that exists. For a typical Adelaide service business, three earn their keep and the rest are noise.

Schema typeWhat it labelsWhy it's worth it
LocalBusinessName, address, phone, hours, area servedThe foundation — tells Google who and where you are
FAQPageCommon questions and your answersCan show your answers directly in search results
Review / AggregateRatingYour star rating and review countPuts the stars on your listing that make people click

Get LocalBusiness right first. It's the one that anchors everything, and it's the one most small sites are missing entirely. The other two are the icing.

You probably don't need to touch code

This is where people psych themselves out. They imagine hand-writing lines of code, breaking the site, and calling a developer in a panic. In reality, most of you are a plugin or a setting away.

If you're on WordPress, an SEO plugin like Yoast or Rank Math writes the important schema automatically once you fill in your business details. Squarespace, Wix and Shopify build a good chunk of it in. Hand-coding a snippet only comes up when you want something specific the platform won't do — and even then it's a paragraph of text dropped into the page, not a rebuild.

The FAQ schema is a nice example: if you've written a genuine questions section on a page — the way we structure the ones at the bottom of these posts — marking it up is a small, tidy job with a real payoff in the results.

How to check it's working

Schema is invisible, which means you can't eyeball it — you have to test it. Google gives you a free tool called the Rich Results Test. Paste in a page URL and it reads the page exactly the way Google does, then tells you which structured data it found, whether it's valid, and what rich results you qualify for.

Do this any time you add or change schema. "Present" and "working" aren't the same thing — a stray typo in the code can quietly break it, and the only way you'd ever know is by testing. Thirty seconds now saves a "why aren't my stars showing" mystery later.

Where it fits in the bigger picture

Schema is a supporting act, not the headliner. It makes the groundwork you've already laid show up better — so it only pays off once that groundwork exists. Your Google Business Profile needs to be sorted, your details need to be consistent everywhere, and you need real reviews before review schema has anything to show.

Once that's in place, schema is how you make sure Google reads all of it correctly, and Search Console is how you watch whether the richer listings start earning you more clicks. If you want the full map of how these pieces fit together, the complete local SEO guide is the place to start.

Add the LocalBusiness schema, test it, and you've done more than most of your competitors bothered to. It's a quiet win — no one will ever compliment your structured data — but it's the kind of tidy foundation that makes everything else work a little harder.
