Why 'we built it on Wix' isn't hurting your SEO (usually)

By the lemonlolly team·26 July 2026·5 min read

It usually arrives as a throwaway line from someone at a barbecue. "Oh, you're on Wix? Yeah, that's probably why you're not showing up on Google." The business owner nods, feels a small pit of dread, and starts wondering whether they need to burn the whole site down and start again.

We hear it constantly from Adelaide business owners, and nine times out of ten it's a red herring. The platform is almost never the reason a site isn't ranking. So before you spend a few thousand dollars rebuilding on something "better", let's be honest about what Wix does, what it doesn't, and where your effort actually pays off.

What Google actually thinks of Wix

Google has been unusually blunt on this one. Its own guidance, repeated by its search staff for years, is that it does not care what you built your site with. It reads the finished page — the words, the structure, the speed, the links pointing at it — and judges that. The tool you used to make the page is invisible to it, the same way a restaurant review judges the meal, not the brand of oven.

So the idea of a "Wix penalty" is a myth. There isn't one, and there never was. Early Wix, a decade ago, genuinely did produce clunky pages that Google struggled to read. That reputation stuck around long after the actual problem was fixed. Today's Wix outputs clean, standard pages that Google crawls without complaint.

If your competitor is beating you on Google, it's almost certainly because they've done the work — not because they picked a different website builder.

Where the myth comes from

Reputations lag reality by years. Wix earned its bad name around 2013 when its pages were built in a way search engines found hard to parse. That's ancient history now, but the folklore outlived the flaw — a bit like people who still think you can't get a good coffee north of the Torrens.

The other reason it persists: it's a convenient excuse. If your rankings are poor, "it's the platform" is a far more comfortable story than "we've never asked for a review, our homepage doesn't say where we are, and the site takes six seconds to load." The platform makes a lovely scapegoat because fixing it feels like someone else's job.

What actually decides your rankings

Here's the honest hierarchy. None of the things that move the needle are platform-specific — you can do every one of them on Wix, Squarespace, WordPress or a hand-coded site.

The handful of real Wix limitations

Being fair to the sceptics, Wix isn't flawless. There are a few genuine constraints — they're just far smaller than the reputation suggests, and none of them are dealbreakers for a typical local business:

  • Less raw speed control. On Wix you can't tinker under the bonnet the way a developer can on a custom build. In practice a tidy Wix site still loads fine — but if you've stuffed it with huge images and heavy apps, you have fewer levers to fix it. Our piece on website speed covers the fixes that are within your control.
  • Some advanced schema is fiddlier. Structured data is doable on Wix, but certain custom markup takes a workaround rather than a clean paste. For most local businesses the built-in options cover it — see schema markup explained simply.
  • Migrating away later is a job. If you ever do outgrow it, moving off Wix isn't a one-click export. Worth knowing going in, not a reason to avoid it.

Notice none of those say "your pages won't rank." They're ceiling issues for large, complex sites — not floor issues for a plumber, a café or a day spa.

When switching platforms is actually worth it

There are real reasons to move — they're just rarely about SEO. Consider a rebuild if you've genuinely outgrown what Wix can do: you're running serious e-commerce with hundreds of products, you need custom functionality Wix can't offer, you're managing thousands of pages, or the site has become such a tangle that starting fresh is cheaper than untangling it.

If that's you, fair enough. But do it with eyes open — and mind the redirects. Rebuilding a site and forgetting to map the old page addresses to the new ones is the single most common way businesses accidentally torch the rankings they already had. That's a genuine SEO risk, and ironically it's caused by leaving Wix, not staying on it.

What to do instead this week

If your Wix site isn't pulling its weight, resist the urge to rebuild and do the unglamorous things first. Claim and fill out your Google Business Profile. Ask three happy customers for a review. Rewrite your homepage so it says clearly what you do and where you do it. Check your page speed and compress the biggest images.

Do those four things and you'll almost always see more movement than any platform migration would have bought you — for a fraction of the cost and none of the risk. The builder was never the problem. The work was just waiting to be done.
