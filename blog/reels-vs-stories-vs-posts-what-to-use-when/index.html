Reels vs Stories vs Posts: what to use when

By the lemonlolly team·20 August 2026·5 min read

You've got a good photo. The finished courtyard, the new coffee blend, the shelves you spent Sunday restocking. You open Instagram — and then you stall. Reel? Story? Just a normal post? You flick a mental coin, put it somewhere, and it does approximately nothing.

That's not a content problem. It's a format problem, and it's one of the most common reasons small businesses feel like social media isn't working for them. Each of the three formats does a completely different job. Once you know which job you're trying to get done, the choice takes about two seconds.

The one question that decides it

Before you pick a format, answer this: who is this actually for?

  • People who've never heard of you → Reel.
  • People who already follow you → Story.
  • People sizing you up before they enquire → feed post.

That's the whole framework. Everything below is just detail on why. If you take nothing else from this, take that.

Reels are for reach

Reels exist because the platforms want to show people things from accounts they don't follow. That's the entire point of the format — it's the discovery engine. If your goal is "more people in Adelaide should know we exist", a Reel is the tool.

What suits the format: a fifteen-second before-and-after, a quick demonstration of something you do well, one tip delivered straight to camera, a walkthrough of the space. Vertical, sound optional, and a hook in the first second — because that first second is genuinely where most people decide.

Here's the honest bit though. Reach is not the same as customers. A Reel that pulls forty thousand views from all over the country is a nice ego hit and does very little for a business that only serves the western suburbs. The numbers worth watching on a Reel are saves, shares and profile visits — those are the ones that mean someone actually cared.

Stories are for the relationship

Stories are seen almost entirely by people who already follow you. They vanish after 24 hours, nobody expects them to be polished, and that low bar is exactly what makes them useful. It's the format where you can be a person rather than a business.

Good Story material is the stuff that's too small to justify a feed post: today's specials, a delivery arriving, a job halfway through, a poll asking which of two colours people prefer, a reminder that you're closed Monday. Add a question sticker or a link sticker and you turn a broadcast into a conversation — and Story replies land in your DMs, which is where a surprising number of enquiries actually start.

Reels get you seen. Stories get you known. Posts get you believed. You need all three, but almost never for the same idea in the same week.

Feed posts are the ones that stay

Your grid is your shopfront. Someone watches a Reel, taps your profile, and then judges you entirely on what they find there. That's the moment the feed post earns its keep.

Feed posts — single images or carousels — are for proof. Finished work, a testimonial, the product on a clean background, a carousel that explains what something costs and why. They don't disappear, they turn up in search and hashtag results months later, and they're what someone scrolls back through at 10pm while deciding whether to message you.

One idea, all three formats

Say you're a landscaper and you've just finished a courtyard in Unley. That's one job and three assets, and the extra effort is about twenty minutes.

  1. Reel: a fifteen-second before-and-after. The "before" shot is your hook — lead with the mess, not the reveal.
  2. Stories: three frames spread across the week. Day one demolition, the pavers arriving, the finished thing — with a link sticker to your enquiry page on the last one.
  3. Feed post: a carousel. Finished shot first, then three close-ups of the detail, and a caption that says roughly what it cost and how long it took. That's the one that's still winning you work in November.

Working this way is also the easiest fix for the "I don't have time" problem — you're not finding three ideas, you're finding one and cutting it three ways. We go deeper on building a rhythm around this in the post on how often a small business should really post.

If you can only manage one thing

Plenty of Adelaide business owners can commit to one format a week and no more. That's fine — just make it the right one for where your business is.

  • New, and nobody knows you exist? Reels. You need reach before anything else matters.
  • Established with regulars who buy often? Stories. Staying front of mind is worth more than new eyeballs.
  • Selling something considered and expensive? Feed posts. People need to see evidence before they'll spend.

The mistakes we see most

  • Putting text in the bottom third of a Reel, where the caption and buttons cover it.
  • Treating Stories as a broadcast channel and never asking anything — no polls, no questions, no replies.
  • Letting the grid go stale because Reels are more fun to make.
  • Chasing a trending sound that has nothing to do with the business.
  • Judging all three formats by the same number. Views mean something very different on a Story than on a Reel.

Where this fits

Format choice sits inside a bigger picture — which platforms you're on at all, what you're actually talking about, and how often. If you've not settled those yet, start with the complete social media strategy for local businesses and come back to this once you know what you're posting about.

But if you already have the ideas and you're just hovering over the share button wondering where the thing should go — ask who it's for. Strangers, followers, or someone deciding whether to trust you. The format picks itself from there.

Format questions, answered

Usually, yes — Reels are the format the platforms actively push to people who don't follow you, so raw view counts tend to be higher. But reach and results aren't the same thing. A Reel seen by ten thousand strangers scattered across the country is worth less to a Norwood florist than a feed post seen by two hundred locals. Judge a Reel by saves, shares and profile visits, not by views.

You can post the same idea in both, but not the same file. A Reel needs a hook in the first second and works vertically with sound off; a feed post needs a strong single image or a carousel that rewards swiping. Same story, two edits. What you shouldn't do is dump a vertical Reel into the grid untouched and wonder why it looks awkward.

Two to five frames is a comfortable range for most small businesses, and they don't need to be posted all at once. Stories are the low-stakes format — nobody expects them to be polished and they vanish in 24 hours, so the bar is much lower than a feed post. The bigger risk is posting none for a fortnight and then twenty in one afternoon.
