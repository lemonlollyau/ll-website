LinkedIn for Adelaide small business owners

By the lemonlolly team·28 August 2026·5 min read

LinkedIn has a reputation problem among small business owners, and it's largely deserved. Open the feed and you'll find someone turning their child's football game into a lesson about resilience in enterprise sales. It's easy to conclude the whole place is nonsense and close the tab.

But underneath the performance, LinkedIn is doing something genuinely useful for Adelaide, which is a referral town. It's a searchable, up-to-date directory of who works where — and in a city this size, three people you already know are usually one degree from the person you actually want to talk to. That's worth twenty minutes a week.

Who this is worth it for

Be honest about whether you're the audience. LinkedIn earns its keep if you sell to other businesses, if your work comes through referrals and professional relationships, if you hire, or if a single customer is worth thousands rather than fifties.

It earns nothing if you're a cafe, a salon or a retailer selling to the general public. Those businesses should be on Instagram and in the suburb Facebook groups, and can ignore LinkedIn entirely with a clear conscience.

Fix your profile, then mostly leave it

Your profile does the work whether you post or not, because people look you up after they meet you. Treat it as a landing page, not a CV.

SectionWhat to do with it
HeadlineSay what you do for whom — not "Director" but "Commercial fit-outs for Adelaide hospitality"
PhotoA clear, recent, friendly one. Not the wedding crop.
BannerYour work, or your logo. It's free real estate most people waste.
AboutFour or five plain sentences: who you help, what you do, how to reach you.
FeaturedA project, a case study, your website. Something to click.

Do it once, properly, and you won't need to touch it for a year. If describing what you do in one line is the hard part, the positioning exercise is the piece that makes it easy.

The three plays that actually work

Connect after you meet people. This is the highest-value habit and it takes ninety seconds. Every event, every meeting, every good phone call — connect the same day with one line reminding them where you met. That's how the network becomes useful rather than decorative.

Ask for warm introductions. When you want to reach a particular business, look at who you both know. A short message to a mutual contact asking whether they'd be comfortable introducing you works startlingly well, and it works because it's the opposite of a cold approach.

Comment more than you post. Thoughtful comments on other people's posts put you in front of their network without you having to produce anything. Fifteen minutes a week of genuinely useful replies will do more for a small business than a monthly essay nobody reads.

Your LinkedIn network is only worth what your last conversation was. Connect on the day you meet someone, or you never will.

If you do post: keep it plain

You don't need to become a content creator. One post a fortnight is plenty, and the ones that work for small businesses are unglamorous:

  • A finished job with a sentence about the problem it solved.
  • Something you learned the hard way, told straight.
  • A useful observation about your industry or the local market.
  • A hire, a milestone, a move — the ordinary business news people are happy to congratulate.

Write like you'd talk. No "thrilled to announce", no one-line-per-paragraph shouting, no manufactured vulnerability. The cringe is optional, and skipping it makes you stand out more than joining in ever would.

What to ignore

Skip LinkedIn Premium unless you're recruiting or doing genuine outbound sales at volume — the free version does everything a small business owner needs. Skip automation tools that send connection requests for you; people can tell, and it burns the relationship you were trying to build. And skip the guilt about not posting daily. Nobody is keeping score.

Your company page, briefly

You probably want one, and you probably shouldn't spend much time on it. A LinkedIn company page is mostly there so your business shows up as a real entity when people search, and so your team can list themselves as working there. It gets far less reach than your personal profile does.

Fill in the description, the logo, the website link and the location, then leave it. If you post, post from your own profile and let people find the company from there — personal accounts consistently reach more people, and on LinkedIn people would rather hear from a human than a brand.

Twenty minutes a week

Here's the whole routine. Ten minutes on Monday connecting with anyone you met last week and replying to messages. Ten minutes on Thursday commenting on a handful of posts from people you'd like to stay near. Post when you've genuinely got something, which might be fortnightly and might be monthly.

It's a slow channel. Nothing happens for months, and then someone you connected with a year ago sends you an enquiry because your name was the one they remembered. In a city where most work arrives through someone knowing someone, that's a reasonable trade for twenty minutes.
