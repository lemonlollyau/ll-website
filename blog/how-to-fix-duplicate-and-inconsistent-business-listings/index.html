How to fix duplicate and inconsistent business listings

By the lemonlolly team·29 July 2026·5 min read

Picture a Norwood café that's been trading for eight years. Somewhere along the way it got listed as "The Corner Café", "Corner Cafe Norwood" and "The Corner Café & Roastery". One listing has the old landline, one has the mobile, and a Yellow Pages entry from three fit-outs ago still shows the address before they moved two doors down. The owner has no idea any of this exists. Google, meanwhile, is quietly unsure whether it's looking at one business or four.

That confusion has a cost, and it's a common one. If your local rankings feel stuck for no obvious reason, mismatched listings are one of the first things worth checking — right up there with the profile fixes in why your Adelaide business isn't showing up on Google Maps.

What NAP consistency actually means

NAP is one of those industry acronyms that sounds fancier than it is. It stands for Name, Address and Phone number — the three details that tell the internet who you are and where to find you. Consistency just means writing them the same way everywhere they appear.

Google builds its confidence in your business by cross-checking those details across the web. When your name, address and phone match everywhere, Google is confident you're one real, legitimate business. When they don't, it hedges — and hedging shows up as weaker local rankings.

Here's how small the differences can be and still cause a wobble:

DetailConsistentInconsistent
NameCorner Café NorwoodThe Corner Cafe & Roastery
AddressUnit 2, 100 The Parade, Norwood SA 5067100 The Parade, Norwood
Phone(08) 8331 20000400 123 456

None of those look like a crisis to a human. But to Google they're three different claims about the same business, and doubt is exactly what you don't want to be feeding it.

Why duplicates and mismatches hold you back

Two problems tend to travel together here, and it's worth separating them.

Inconsistency is when the same business is described slightly differently across listings. It dilutes trust and makes Google work harder to be sure of you.

Duplication is when the same business has two or more separate listings — often a second Google profile someone created without realising one already existed. This is the more damaging of the two, because it splits everything you've earned: reviews on one, clicks on the other, and neither profile as strong as a single combined one would be.

Every duplicate listing is a second front door that some of your customers — and some of your ranking signals — walk through instead of the one you actually maintain.

How to find every listing (the audit)

You can't fix what you can't see, so the first job is a stocktake. Set aside an hour and work through this:

  • Search your business name in Google, then search it again with your suburb. Note every listing that appears — website, Google profile, Facebook, directories, the lot.
  • Search your phone number in quotes, like "(08) 8331 2000". This surfaces old listings that used a different business name but the same number — the sneaky ones.
  • Search your street address the same way to catch listings tied to a previous name or owner.
  • Check the big directories directly — Yellow Pages, True Local, Yelp, Hotfrog and your industry's association pages.
  • Write it all down. A simple spreadsheet with columns for the site, the name shown, the address, the phone and whether it's right or wrong beats trying to hold it in your head.

By the end you'll have a map: the listings that are correct, the ones that need editing, and the duplicates that need to go.

Fixing the mismatches

Before you touch anything, decide on your one true version of your name, address and phone. Write it down exactly — capitalisation, punctuation, the "Unit 2", the phone format. This is your source of truth, and every listing gets edited to match it, character for character.

Then work through the list from your audit:

  1. Start with the ones you own. Your website, Google Business Profile and Facebook are under your control — bring them into line first. Your Google Business Profile is the most important single listing, so get that spot-on.
  2. Claim the listings you don't own yet. Most directories let you claim a business to edit it. It's tedious but it's the only way to correct them properly.
  3. Update, don't duplicate. When you find a wrong listing, edit it — resist the urge to create a fresh one and leave the old one floating, because that just adds another duplicate to clean up later.
  4. Match your true version exactly. Not "close enough". If your address says "Unit 2" everywhere else, it says "Unit 2" here too.

Killing duplicates the right way

Duplicates need care rather than a delete key. On Google, sign in, find the extra profile and use the option to report it as a duplicate — Google will typically merge or retire it and roll the history into your main listing, which is exactly what you want. Don't just delete a duplicate that has reviews on it; report it so those reviews have a chance of migrating across.

On directories, claim the duplicate and either correct it or ask the site to remove it. Some are slower to respond than others, so keep a note of what you've requested and chase the stragglers. It's not glamorous work, but it's the kind of quiet housekeeping that pays off for years.

Keeping it consistent from here on

Once you're clean, staying clean is easy — as long as you're deliberate about it. Keep your one true version of your details in a note somewhere obvious. Any time you add a new listing or someone asks for your details for a directory, copy from that note rather than typing from memory. And if you move, change your number or rebrand, treat updating every listing as part of the job, not an afterthought.

Consistent listings won't rocket you to the top on their own. But they clear away the doubt that holds a lot of Adelaide businesses back — and they're the foundation the rest of your local SEO is built on.
